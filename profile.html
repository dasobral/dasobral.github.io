Profile / Daniel Sobral Blanco

A physicist’s curiosity.
An engineer’s craft.

I’m a software engineer and physicist, interested in how careful models become useful systems—and how we know those systems deserve our trust.

Currently an Application Engineer at Quside, based in Madrid.

01 / Background

Following the
interesting questions.

My path started in fundamental physics in Madrid and continued in Geneva, where I completed a PhD in theoretical physics. My research explored relativistic effects in large-scale structure: subtle signals that can help us test gravity and understand the Universe.

That work brought mathematics and computation together. It also shaped how I approach engineering: make assumptions explicit, build models that can be tested, and distinguish a promising idea from a demonstrated result.

Today, I work at the intersection of quantum-safe communications, software engineering, and scientific computing. My public projects extend that curiosity into agent authorization, developer tooling, and reproducible optimization experiments.

02 / Working principles

Clarity before
confidence.

  1. Make the assumptions visible.

    A system is easier to trust when its boundaries, failure modes, and underlying assumptions are explicit.

  2. Connect theory to implementation.

    A mathematical idea becomes more useful when it can be examined in working software, with reproducible experiments and clear interfaces.

  3. Let the evidence set the claim.

    Research prototypes, benchmarks, and production systems answer different questions. I try to be precise about what each result establishes.
